# jAI demo script

Presenter notes for Jason Wood. About 13 minutes before questions, 15 if the room is talkative. On-screen cues are still marked draft in code until you sign off on the words.

Use **Staff** view. Client view hides account spend, and the investor market figures are easier to read in Staff. Every account on screen is sample data. Say that out loud once, then the badge keeps saying it.

Do not name other companies. Meta, Google, TikTok, and StackAdapt are delivery channels. Do not say bots, duplicates, or stale IDs are filtered out before a dollar is spent. Do not quote a Specificity revenue, customer, or margin number. The boxes say `[Specificity figure]` until you put a counsel-reviewed number there. Do not promise a return.

## Open (before the clock)

Home is the command center. Point at the logo and the line under it: "Your audience. Everywhere." and "Bots optimize, you make the call."

Click **Start the tour**. Arrow keys move a present-mode tour. Escape exits.

## 0:00–2:00 The problem

Screen: `/demo/problem`

Say: "Unprotected, platform-guessed buying is where non-human traffic shows up. These are published research figures, not our results, and they measure different things, so I will not add them."

Read the large figure, then the three cards, including the small source line on each:

- Thales 2026 Bad Bot Report, April 29, 2026: bots were 53% of web traffic in 2025, bad bots 40%.
- Pixalate, Q2 2026 (published August 12, 2026): US programmatic invalid traffic was 25% on web, 39% on mobile app, and 25% on CTV.
- ANA / TAG TrustNet / Fiducia, Q2 2026: 45.1% of programmatic spend reached viewable, fraud-free quality impressions.
- DoubleVerify 2026 Global Insights, July 2026: 2.8% fraud on unprotected campaigns, 0.6% on protected ones.

Then the small line at the bottom: we do not show pre-serve bot filtering, and no verification partner is connected. What we show next is a traceable list, a conversion that has to pass a contact check, and a budget write a bot cannot send.

## 2:00–3:30 Live data market, not a stale database

Screen: `/demo/audiences`

Say: "An owned file goes stale. The data market moves. For this sample campaign we source a fresh list. Every row has the vendor, the list id, and the time it was sourced."

Point at Harbor List Co (Demo), list `demo-list-harbor-041`, sourced October 28, 2026. Then the older card: the owned file from 2024 is on screen and not used. 80 sample ids, 48 marked matched. That match mark is the sample file, not a live platform report.

## 3:30–5:00 One traceable audience, everywhere

Screen: `/demo/serving`

Say: "Same vendor. Same list id. Prepared for Meta, Google, TikTok, and StackAdapt. Pixels can retarget people who engage. This demo does not call a live platform. A person turns a live upload on."

On the Google card, read the policy line: brokered lists are a Google Customer Match policy risk, and this demo does not upload them. The table underneath is the rest of the sample workspace. If you are in Client view, spend is off that table.

## 5:00–6:00 Segments compete

Screen: `/demo/segments`

Say: "The same audience can be sliced. This seed marks one segment to scale, one to keep in test, and one to cut. The label is a suggestion. A person still decides."

## 6:00–7:30 Verified vs platform-reported

Screen: `/demo/verified`

Say: "Platforms report every conversion they see. We count one only when it came from a data-market MAID campaign, passed a contact check, and is not a duplicate. These rows were run through that rule. They are sample rows, not a client result."

Point at the gap: reported, verified, and not counted. Read one duplicate, one failed contact check, and one platform-reported row.

## 7:30–8:15 Beside platform targeting

Screen: `/demo/compare`

Say: "Our audience sits beside platform targeting. There are no figures on this step. It is a picture of the difference in method, and it is labeled illustrative."

Do not turn it into a scoreboard.

## 8:15–9:00 Plain English

Screen: `/demo/explanations`

Say: "Every note says what we would do, and it ends with Based on and the seeded row. You can see the reason without a dashboard translation."

## 9:00–10:30 Bots optimize, you make the call

Screen: `/demo/decisions`

Say: "A bot just tried to write a daily cap on the sample TikTok campaign. The same lock the product uses blocked it. The bot can recommend. It cannot send the change."

Read the blocked message. Then click **Approve**. Say: "That approval is a person. The bot still did not send it. This demo does not change a platform setting." Decline works the same way. The feed under the card is the rest of the sample inbox.

## 10:30–11:45 Vendor honesty checks

Screen: `/demo/vendors`

Say: "We mix known fake IDs into a vendor list. They are not real devices, and they are not sent. A vendor that matches too many of them is flagged. A holdout group sees no ads, and lift is measured against that group."

Point at Harbor List Co, which stays inside the check, and Gulf Data Desk (Demo), which is flagged. The matched-fake count on the flagged list is a sample input. The holdout sentence is the product's math on sample inputs. Say it is not a client result. Nothing is sent. The check recommends only.

## 11:45–12:30 The client report

Screen: `/demo/report`

Say: "This is the weekly note, in plain English. Booked visits lead. There is a Based on line. Cost stays off the client view. Staff see seed spend, labeled illustrative. The page does not send a change."

## 12:30–15:00 Pick an ending

Screen: `/demo/close`

Say: "Same tour up to here. I pick the ending for the room." Click one card, or use the buttons on the presenter bar. You can switch from the ending screen.

Screen: `/demo/investor`

Read the two market figures and their source line: US internet advertising $294.6B in 2025, and US programmatic $162.4B, IAB/PwC Internet Ad Revenue Report, April 16, 2026. Say these are public market figures, not Specificity results.

Point at the proof links back to the screens you already showed.

Why now, in four sentences: bots were a majority of observed web traffic in 2025; unprotected programmatic buying is where invalid traffic concentrates; identity stays split across walled gardens, so a fresh device-level audience still matters; automated buying is spreading, and our rule is structural.

Read the forward-looking-statements box out loud, including that it is subject to review by securities counsel and that it does not promise any return. Do not add an ask, a valuation, or a projection that is not on the screen.

- "Are these match rates live?" No. The demo does not call Meta, Google, TikTok, or StackAdapt.
- "What do you charge?" `[Specificity figure]`. Not a quote.
- "What returns should I expect?" None are promised. The investor box says that, and counsel reviews it before it is used with investors.
